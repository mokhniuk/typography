Where our typography engine stands against six others

The same 250 scored cases (English and 25 other target languages), the same inputs and the same machine, for typography-engine (this repository, 0.15.0) against typograf, typopo, richtypo, tipograph, smartypants and retext-smartypants. 0.2.0 closed most of the gaps the first version of this page found: US quotes, 20 more languages, opt-in rules (ten of the thirty-three today), a custom rule API, digit grouping, and a 10× faster engine. Read the top for the verdict, the middle for the evidence, and the bottom for what is still open.

Where we lead

  • Rule quality on the benchmark. Highest pass count and pass rate overall (a few groups, such as plain quotes, are close or led by a tool with fewer cases), with full marks on ranges, abbreviations and no-break spaces; the only tool that binds J. R. R. and Dr. Smith. The cases were written by the author of one of the tools: read the caveat below.
  • Language coverage. A pack for each of the 27 target languages (26 with cases; the two Swiss packs have none), and the only tool with Croatian, Lithuanian or Maltese.
  • Speed. Faster than typograf, the other tool with a full rule set, at every size from 10 KB.
  • Safe on code and links. URLs, emails and code spans come back byte for byte; four tools damage at least one of them.
  • Everything around the engine. HTML, Markdown, rehype, React, a CLI, and hanging punctuation measured in the browser.
  • Trust. Property tests prove no letter is lost and a second run changes nothing.

Where we trail

  • English quotes. English is “…” with ‘…’ inside by default since 0.15.0; quoteStyle: 'guillemets' keeps the house style «…».
  • Languages are unreviewed. Every pack has reviewed: false; twenty were written from style guides, some read only in part. A defensible style, not a certified one. A list of known gaps is in the backlog.
  • Opt-in rules. Fractions, ±, °C, space before punctuation, trailing-space cleanup and phone numbers exist but are off by default: ten of the thirty-three. Short words (to~the) bind by default since 0.14.0.
  • Speed and distribution. Still behind tipograph and smartypants, which do far less; no hard input is quadratic since 0.15.0. Not on npm, no licence, sources shipped as TypeScript (deferred by choice).
  • Weight. 26.5 KB brotli for one call, against typograf's 15: every entry point carries all 27 language packs (16.4 KB of it). An install from a tag is 337 KB from the next release (it was 3.9 MB, the whole repository).

Speed

How fast each one is

English prose, text mode; the 1 KB bar is time per call (bun run latency), the others are best of three, one run at 1 MB (bun run bench). Bars use a logarithmic axis, so each gridline is ten times the one before. Hatched bars are estimates: the tool was too slow to run at that size. The 100 KB and 1 MB bars are the 0.15.0 run (2026-10-23): typography-engine 49 ms, typograf 71 ms at 1 MB, typopo measured in full at 122 s; the 1 KB bar is the earlier latency run, not re-run. Two things moved since the 0.12.0 run (30 against 58): the short-word rule runs by default now, about 8 ms per MB of English, and the machine differs between runs (the 0.14.0 run was about twice as slow for every tool, 74 against 115). Read the ratios between the bars, not the milliseconds. 0.12.0 also removed a cost no benchmark text shows: the zone scan (code spans, addresses, emails set aside before the rules) took 300 ms per MB on text with lone backticks and no line break, from a regex flag it did not need; it is under 1 ms now. space.number became one scan instead of three passes.

Worst case, 0.2.1. A run of 40,000 opening quote marks went from 59 s to 7 ms. Dialogue and unbalanced text went from 180 to 200 ms/MB to 45 to 70 ms/MB. 20,000 characters of x, digits or - went from 360 to 640 ms to under 3 ms; the cause was the email regex of the zone shield, tried from every position. No superlinear input was known in the rules then; the hard-input probes below found two outside them.

Cost

What each one costs to ship and run

New on 2026-10-22, from bun run size, deps, engines and probes in the benchmark. One call that typesets English, bundled for the browser with every dependency inlined and minified, then compressed. Typography-engine carries all 27 language packs whichever is asked for: 16.4 of its 26.5 KB brotli are the packs.

Other engines. The same bundles under Bun 1.3.11 (JavaScriptCore, as Safari), Node 24.14.1 and Deno 2.9.3 (V8, as Chrome), each in a fresh process: a warm call on 1 KB, 100 KB in one piece, and the peak memory of a process that typesets 1 MB. Typography-engine and typograf keep their order on every engine. richtypo is the outlier: about 25 times slower on JavaScriptCore than on V8.

Hard inputs. About 10 KB built to find a slow path (long runs of one mark, a 10,000-letter word, deep or broken HTML), each in its own process, stopped at 10 s. Typography-engine stays under 8 ms on all twelve. The two that were quadratic in 0.14.0 are linear since 0.15.0: a long run of spaces inside a line in the text mode (1.7 ms, from 78 ms) and many < that never close in the HTML mode (2.9 ms, from 106 ms; it was 200 ms at 4,000 repeats and 800 ms at 8,000). richtypo takes seconds on nine of them, typopo stops at 10 s on one. German prose also costs typography-engine about three times English (13 ms against 5 per 100 KB), a lead to follow.

Why

Why each tool is fast or slow

Counts are from each package's shipped source (typography-engine: every \p{ and (?< in src/core, tests excluded, comments and language packs included; 103 and 42 at 0.12.0). The counts did not change the speed of typography-engine: 0.2.0 kept most of its Unicode classes and lookbehinds and went from 324 ms to 33 ms. What matters is whether a regex opens with a lookbehind, which the engine tries at every position; \p{L} itself was not the cause. The old figures for 1 MB on 0.1.1 were 0.5 ms for a trivial pass and 27 ms for one lookbehind-led \p{L} pass.

Our engine

Where our time goes now

Time per rule on 1 MB of English as one paragraph, from bun run profile; share of the sum (47 ms, the 0.12.0 run). The profile runs every rule, including the eleven that are off by default, and since 0.12.0 it also times the zone scan as a row of its own and runs on the fuzz corpus as well as prose (neither shows here: the scan is under 1 ms). space.number fell from 10 to 6% when its three passes became one. Compare the shares, not the milliseconds. The opt-in space.before-punctuation and punctuation.double now cost about 1 to 2 ms each, and symbol.degree 5 ms (in 0.2.0 they were 18, 16 and 20 ms). Cost is linear, and no rule stands far above the rest.

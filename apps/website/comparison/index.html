Where our typography engine stands against six others

The same 260 scored cases (English and 24 other target languages), the same inputs and the same machine, for @mokhniuk/typography (this repository, 0.18.0) against typograf, typopo, richtypo, tipograph, smartypants and retext-smartypants. 0.2.0 closed most of the gaps the first version of this page found: US quotes, 20 more languages, opt-in rules ({{rulesOff}} of the {{rules}} today), a custom rule API, digit grouping, and a 10× faster engine. Read the top for the verdict, the middle for the evidence, and the bottom for the notes and the method.

Where we lead

  • Rule quality on the benchmark. Highest pass count and pass rate overall (a few groups, such as plain quotes, are close or led by a tool with fewer cases), with full marks on ranges, abbreviations and no-break spaces; the only tool that binds J. R. R. and Dr. Smith. The cases were written by the author of one of the tools: read the caveat below.
  • Language coverage. A pack for each of the 27 target languages (25 with cases; the two Swiss packs have none), and the only tool with Croatian, Lithuanian or Maltese.
  • Speed. Faster than typograf, the other tool with a full rule set, at every size from 10 KB.
  • Safe on code and links. URLs, emails and code spans come back byte for byte; four tools damage at least one of them.
  • Everything around the engine. HTML, Markdown, rehype, React, a CLI, and hanging punctuation measured in the browser.
  • Trust. Property tests prove no letter is lost and a second run changes nothing.

What we are improving

  • Native-speaker review. Every pack has reviewed: false. Twenty were written from style guides, some read only in part, and none has had a native reader go through its page. That review comes first, and each language gets its flag only after it.
  • Sources. 16 of the {{rules}} rule decisions still rest on general knowledge, and two (quote and space.percent) disagree with CLDR for some languages. The plan is to read the sources, then upgrade or change each decision.
  • Language gaps. No dialogue dash for Greek and Latvian, the Spanish and Nordic dash before the narrator, the Irish pack is thin, and the short-word lists are editorial in five packs and absent from the other twenty-two.
  • Lighter entries. A page that sets one language already imports /lite and its pack (about 12 KB brotli, against 30 KB for the full entry). The rehype, React, markdown-it and Vue entries still carry all {{langs}} packs, and the pack prose that the engine never reads still ships; both are planned.
  • More places to use it. Plugins for Figma and Penpot are coming; an Astro integration, a Vue component with server rendering, Svelte helpers and a remark plugin are next if people ask.
  • A bigger benchmark. English has 97 of the 262 cases and most other languages six. Real-text cases per language, written by a native reader, would make the scores harder to argue with.

Speed

How fast each one is

English prose, text mode; the 1 KB bar is time per call (bun run latency), the others are the median of seven runs, three at 1 MB (bun run bench). Bars use a logarithmic axis, so each gridline is ten times the one before. Hatched bars are estimates: the tool was too slow to run at that size. The 100 KB and 1 MB bars are the 0.16.1 run (2026-10-23): @mokhniuk/typography 38 ms, typograf 58 ms at 1 MB, typopo measured in full at 99 s; the 1 KB bar is the earlier latency run, not re-run. Two things moved since the 0.12.0 run (30 against 58): the short-word rule runs by default now, about 8 ms per MB of English, and the machine differs between runs (the 0.14.0 run was about twice as slow for every tool, 74 against 115). Read the ratios between the bars, not the milliseconds. 0.12.0 also removed a cost no benchmark text shows: the zone scan (code spans, addresses, emails set aside before the rules) took 300 ms per MB on text with lone backticks and no line break, from a regex flag it did not need; it is under 1 ms now. space.number became one scan instead of three passes.

Worst case, 0.2.1. A run of 40,000 opening quote marks went from 59 s to 7 ms. Dialogue and unbalanced text went from 180 to 200 ms/MB to 45 to 70 ms/MB. 20,000 characters of x, digits or - went from 360 to 640 ms to under 3 ms; the cause was the email regex of the zone shield, tried from every position. No superlinear input was known in the rules then; the hard-input probes below found two outside them.

Cost

What each one costs to ship and run

New on 2026-10-22, from bun run size, deps, engines and probes in the benchmark. One call that typesets English, bundled for the browser with every dependency inlined and minified, then compressed. Typography-engine's full entries carry all 27 language packs whichever is asked for: 18.9 of its 30.0 KB brotli are the packs. Since 0.17.0 the same call from /lite with the one pack it uses (/lang/en) is 11.7 KB brotli, 33.9 KB minified; the engine alone is 11.1 KB and a pack adds about 0.4 to 1.5 KB (English 0.6, Ukrainian 1.1).

Other engines. The same bundles under Bun 1.3.11 (JavaScriptCore, as Safari), Node 24.14.1 and Deno 2.9.3 (V8, as Chrome), each in a fresh process: a warm call on 1 KB, 100 KB in one piece, and the peak memory of a process that typesets 1 MB. Typography-engine and typograf keep their order on every engine. richtypo is the outlier: about 40 times slower on JavaScriptCore than on V8.

Hard inputs. About 10 KB built to find a slow path (long runs of one mark, a 10,000-letter word, deep or broken HTML), each in its own process, stopped at 10 s. Typography-engine stays under 5 ms on all twelve (4.7 ms at most). The two that were quadratic in 0.14.0 are linear since 0.15.0: a long run of spaces inside a line in the text mode (1.5 ms, from 78 ms) and many < that never close in the HTML mode (2.3 ms, from 106 ms; it was 200 ms at 4,000 repeats and 800 ms at 8,000). richtypo takes seconds on eight of them, typopo stops at 10 s on one. German prose cost @mokhniuk/typography about three times English (13 ms against 5 per 100 KB) until 0.16.1: the turned quotation pairs »…« were matched with a regex that opened with a lookbehind; matched forward, German takes 4 ms per 100 KB, the same as English.

Why

Why each tool is fast or slow

Counts are from each package's shipped source (@mokhniuk/typography: every \p{ and (?< in src/core, tests excluded, comments and language packs included; 103 and 42 at 0.12.0). The counts did not change the speed of @mokhniuk/typography: 0.2.0 kept most of its Unicode classes and lookbehinds and went from 324 ms to 33 ms. What matters is whether a regex opens with a lookbehind, which the engine tries at every position; \p{L} itself was not the cause. The old figures for 1 MB on 0.1.1 were 0.5 ms for a trivial pass and 27 ms for one lookbehind-led \p{L} pass.

Our engine

Where our time goes now

Time per rule on 1 MB of English as one paragraph, from bun run profile; share of the sum (47 ms, the 0.12.0 run). The profile runs every rule, including the ten that are off by default, and since 0.12.0 it also times the zone scan as a row of its own and runs on the fuzz corpus as well as prose (neither shows here: the scan is under 1 ms). space.number fell from 10 to 6% when its three passes became one. Compare the shares, not the milliseconds. The opt-in space.before-punctuation and punctuation.double now cost about 1 to 2 ms each, and symbol.degree 5 ms (in 0.2.0 they were 18, 16 and 20 ms). Cost is linear, and no rule stands far above the rest.

Method. Versions: @mokhniuk/typography 0.18.0 (benchmarked; 0.19.0 adds integrations and no output change), typograf 7.8.0, typopo 3.1.0, richtypo 8.0.0, tipograph 0.7.4, smartypants 0.2.2, retext-smartypants 6.2.0. Quality: apps/bench/RESULTS.md, 260 scored cases plus 2 register cases, 25 languages; a tool is scored only on what it supports. Samples: apps/bench/features.ts. Speed: bun run bench for 10 KB to 1 MB, bun run latency for the 1 KB call, bun run profile for the rules; one machine and three runs at 1 MB, so treat speed ratios as approximate. Cost: bun run size (Bun.build, browser, minified, gzip 9 and brotli 11), bun run deps (packages and bytes under node_modules), bun run engines (the same bundles in fresh processes, medians), bun run probes (10 KB hard inputs, 10 s limit).

Not measured. Speed in a real browser (the engines table runs Bun, Node and Deno outside one), and whether a native reader would accept each non-English output (none of our packs has been reviewed). We did not profile typograf, typopo or tipograph beyond the counts in the table, so the reasons given for them are inferences. richtypo's cause (a lookbehind that rescans back to the last <) is read from its source.

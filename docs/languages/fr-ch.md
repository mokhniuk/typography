## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word (`space.short-word`, on by default; the pack's own list): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: yes.
- The space before `:` and before `; ! ?` (`space.high-punctuation`; the opt-in `space.before-punctuation` keeps it): none.
- A bare temperature without the degree sign (`20 C`) read as one (`symbol.degree`, opt-in): no.
- Abbreviations: no list.

## Numbers

Groups of three are separated by `~`, the decimal mark is `.`.

## Notes

Swiss French quotes: `«outer ‹inner› outer»`, without the no-break space inside that the fr pack sets, as the former ch pack set them from the Swiss Federal Chancellery Schreibweisungen (the German edition; no French Swiss guide was read). CLDR has the same marks for fr-CH. No space is set before `; ! ?` and `:`: the pack field that sets it (highPunctuation, read by space.high-punctuation) is set by the fr pack only (this pack copies the fr fields it takes one by one, and not that one). Open item: no Swiss French source on it was read. The Federal Chancellery’s French instructions (May 2016) are silent on `: ; ! ?`; the hints disagree (typoguide.ch is said to set a fine space before `; : ? !`, seen as a summary only; Lacroux mentions Romands who set the fine space; the Chancellery’s own text is typeset with a full space before `:` and `;`). To read before setting the field here: the Guide du typographe (Groupe de Lausanne de l’Association suisse des typographes, the Guide du typographe romand). Dialogue is quoted here (no dialogueDash), unlike the fr pack: the OQLF source on dialogue dashes is cited with the fr sources, but no dialogue dash is set, and either choice is unverified for Switzerland. The no-break space before % and € is French’s (the fr pack), not checked against a Swiss guide. Digits are grouped with a no-break space (number.group) and the decimal mark is a point, as the Schreibweisungen set for federal texts (Rz. 512, 514, 543 for francs); those passages were read in the German edition, from its index and the PDF text, not on the page itself, and the pack stays reviewed: false. Everyday Swiss usage often writes the apostrophe (1’000’000); it is not used here.

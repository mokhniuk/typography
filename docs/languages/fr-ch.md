## Quotation

| Register | Levels, outermost first |
|---|---|
| display | `«…»`, `‹…›` |
| literary | as display |

A quotation nested deeper than the levels listed starts over from the first.

An apostrophe before a dropped-letter word is not a quotation mark: `'em`, `'til`, `'till`, `'cause`, `'bout`, `'round`, `'tis`, `'twas`, `'cos`, `'n'`.

## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word (`space.short-word`, on by default; the pack's own list): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: yes.
- The space before `:` and before `; ! ?` (`space.high-punctuation`; the opt-in `space.before-punctuation` keeps it): none.
- A bare temperature without the degree sign (`20 C`) read as one (`symbol.degree`, opt-in): no.
- Abbreviations: no list.

## Numbers

Groups of three are separated by `~`, the decimal mark is `,` (also `.` in amounts).

## Notes

Swiss French quotes: `«outer ‹inner› outer»`, without the no-break space inside that the fr pack sets, as the former ch pack set them from the Swiss Federal Chancellery Schreibweisungen (the German edition, Rz. 202-203). The Chancellery’s French instructions (May 2016, read in full) say nothing on quotation marks; CLDR has the same marks for fr-CH. No space is set before `; ! ?` and `:`: the pack field that sets it (highPunctuation, read by space.high-punctuation) is set by the fr pack only (this pack copies the fr fields it takes one by one, and not that one). Open item: no Swiss French source on it was read. The Federal Chancellery’s French instructions (May 2016) are silent on `: ; ! ?`; the hints disagree (typoguide.ch is said to set a fine space before `; : ? !`, seen as a summary only; Lacroux mentions Romands who set the fine space; the Chancellery’s own text is typeset with a full space before `:` and `;`). To read before setting the field here: the Guide du typographe (Groupe de Lausanne de l’Association suisse des typographes, the Guide du typographe romand). Dialogue is quoted here (no dialogueDash), unlike the fr pack: the OQLF source on dialogue dashes is cited with the fr sources, but no dialogue dash is set, and either choice is unverified for Switzerland. Numbers, from the French instructions (ch. 4): numbers of more than four digits are grouped in threes with an espace insécable (55 000; 1700 stays whole; number.group), and "l’utilisation de l’apostrophe dans les nombres n’est pas admise" (note 4), so everyday Swiss 1’000’000 is not used. The decimal mark is a comma (4,16 millions de francs). The French instructions write francs and centimes as 23 fr. 50 ("et non fr. 50.-", ch. 2); the German Schreibweisungen set a point between francs and rappen in amounts (Rz. 514, 543: Fr. 327.65) and never group with a point or a comma (Rz. 512). So number.group reads a point as a decimal mark too (numbers.decimal ",."), never a separator: CHF 1234567.50 becomes CHF 1 234 567.50, 1234567,50 becomes 1 234 567,50. Percent and currency: an espace insécable between a number and its unit (ch. 5: 3 %, 50 francs, 300 CHF; 26,1 %), so percentSpace and currencySpace are set. CLDR 48 disagrees on %: its fr-CH percent pattern is "#,##0%" without a space; the Chancellery is followed here. The pack stays reviewed: false (no native reader).

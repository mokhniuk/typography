## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word (`space.short-word`, on by default; the pack's own list): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: no.
- The space before `:` and before `; ! ?` (`space.high-punctuation`; the opt-in `space.before-punctuation` keeps it): none.
- A bare temperature without the degree sign (`20 C`) read as one (`symbol.degree`, opt-in): no.
- Abbreviations: no list.

## Numbers

Groups of three are separated by `~`, the decimal mark is `.`.

## Notes

Quotation: the pack follows the Publications Office table (`‘…’` then `“…”` then `‘…’`, the English order). The Irish edition of the Interinstitutional Style Guide (Treoir Stíle Idirinstitiúideach, section 4.2.3) could not be read: style-guide.europa.eu is script-rendered, and the Cellar lists the Irish PDF (manifestations 0018.01 to 0018.03 of 01ed788a-d266-11ec-a95f-01aa75ed71a1) but serves no file for it (404 on DOC_1 and DOC_2). Wikipedia gives `“…”` first for Irish; quoteStyle: 'us' gives that order. Apostrophe: d', b', m' (d'fhág, b'fhéidir, m'athair) stand inside a word, which the engine already reads as an apostrophe, not a quote mark; the goldens in ga.test.ts pin that inside and outside a quotation, including a quotation written with straight single marks. The elisions field is empty: no Irish word starts with an apostrophe in any source read. Numbers: a point for decimals and a space between groups of three digits are read in the Maltese edition of the ISG, which states it for Irish; the Irish edition was not read. The group separator is a no-break space. Euro: the Maltese edition says Irish writes the euro sign before the amount without a space (€30). The pack sets percentSpace (5% becomes 5 %) but not currencySpace, so no space is added before €: 30€ stays 30€ and €30 is untouched. Not implemented, not verified: one-letter words to keep off the end of a line (none sourced), abbreviation lists (none read), the dot-above letters of the old orthography and the fada need no rule (they are letters; tests pin that they are kept), the Irish dash conventions (not read), and everything in the Irish edition of the ISG. The task brief says typograf covers Irish; this pack was not compared with it.

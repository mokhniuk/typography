## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word (`space.short-word`, on by default; the pack's own list): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: yes.
- A space kept before `: ; ! ?` (`space.before-punctuation`, opt-in): no.
- A bare temperature without the degree sign (`20 C`) read as one (`symbol.degree`, opt-in): no.

## Notes

Dialogue: narrative dialogue may open with a spaced lineetta, and the same spaced dash sets off the narrator (F. Longo, Treccani Magazine 2020, "Lineette doppie e lineetta singola"); `« »` is an equally standard choice. The examples there set the en dash; the pack keeps one dash for the text, the em dash of ISG 10, so dialogue and the narrator match. Quotation: `«…»` then `“…”` then `‘…’` is the order of the Manuale interistituzionale (4.2.3) and of Treccani; some Italian publishers write `“…”` first, with `‘…’` inside, which quoteStyle: 'us' gives. The Accademia della Crusca was searched, but its editorial criteria PDF could not be read, so it is not cited. Apostrophe: the engine already reads an apostrophe inside a word (l’amico, dell’architetto), a closing apostrophe of a truncation (un po’) and a year of two digits (’48) as apostrophes, not quote marks; the goldens in it.test.ts pin that inside and outside a quotation. Truncations: the truncations field lists the words that end in an apostrophe, da’ di’ fa’ sta’ va’ (imperatives) and po’ be’ mo’ pie’ que’, from Treccani, Enciclopedia dell’italiano, entries apostrofo (Definizione, Casi incerti, Errori comuni) and troncamento; both entries were read as summaries of a fetch tool, not on the page itself, so the list is to be checked against the pages. After one of them an apostrophe followed by a space, punctuation or the end is an apostrophe when a later straight single mark can close the quotation, so a quotation in straight single quotes that holds one pairs: 'dammi un po' di pane' gives «dammi un po’ di pane», and 'un po'' gives «un po’». Left out: ve’ and vo’, which Treccani calls archaic or local, and to’, read only on Wiktionary. With no such later mark the quotation closes at the word, as before: 'ha detto di' and 'da' give «ha detto di» and «da». Known limit: da, di, fa and va are also words without an apostrophe, so a quotation that ends on one of them and is followed in the same paragraph by a lone straight single mark that can close, 'ha detto di' e poi basta.', reads the first mark as the apostrophe di’. The elisions field lists only words that start with an apostrophe, and the only ones read in a source are the dropped digits of a year, so it is empty: ’800 and ’900 are not implemented, not verified (a single university handout lists them; no authoritative source was read). Not implemented, not verified: the ISG gives half a fixed space between digit groups (a thin space), set here as a no-break space; no source read gives a list of one-letter words (a, e, i, o) to keep off the end of a line, so there are no orphans; the lineetta of 10.1.10 is the em dash, which is the default; the abbreviation lists are only a part of annex A3, chosen for a number or a second part that follows them.

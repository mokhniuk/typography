## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word, in a title (the opt-in rule `space.short-word`): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: yes.

## Notes

Dialogue: a replikstreg, the same form as the tankestreg (the long dash, see above), may open a line of dialogue (Retskrivningsordbogen 5.1, § 59); quotes or no marks are equally allowed, and the attribution follows a comma. Quotes: ISG 4.2.3 says Danish uses the same marks at every level, `»…«`, and 10.1 gives the nested form `»…»…«…«`. The mark pairs are read by position (turnedPairs), as in German. The other Danish convention, `„…“` (and `‚…‘` inside), which Dansk Sprognævn also accepts, is not implemented and was not read at its source. The sentence dash is the long dash with spaces (ISG 6.4 table and 10.1: "som tankestreg bruges den lange"), which is the engine default; the en dash is the minus and the range sign. Abbreviations: only those the ISG itself writes before a figure or an amount (10.2: s. 50, stk. 3, kl. 17.30; 4.2.3: nr., s.; 10.1: mio. EUR; annex A3: mio., mia.). Danish abbreviations such as f.eks., bl.a., dvs., osv. are written without inner spaces (10.3, A3), so there is nothing to keep together. Not implemented, not verified: one-letter words (the ISG gives no list), the dash rules of Retskrivningsordbogen (Dansk Sprognævn was not read), % before a currency (the ISG writes EUR after a figure, 10.2, with an ordinary space).

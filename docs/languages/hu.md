## Quotation

| Register | Levels, outermost first |
|---|---|
| display | `„…”`, `»…«`, `’…’` |
| literary | as display |

A quotation nested deeper than the levels listed starts over from the first.

Read by where each mark stands, not only by shape: `»…«` (turned the other way round from the usual guillemets).

## Dashes

| What | Setting |
|---|---|
| break in the sentence | spaced en dash |
| break in the sentence, literary | spaced en dash |
| line of dialogue (a paragraph opening with a dash) | en dash and a no-break space, in both registers |

## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: no.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: no.
- Abbreviations: no list.

## Numbers

Groups of three are separated by `~`, the decimal mark is `,`.

## Notes

Quotes: „…” with »…« inside and ’…’ at the third level (EU Publications Office table); a Hungarian journal guide that follows A magyar helyesírás szabályai, 12th ed., gives „idézet »belső idézet« idézet vége”. The MTA rules themselves could not be read. Numbers: a no-break space groups thousands and the decimal mark is a comma (Commission web-writing guide). Not implemented, not verified: % and currency spacing, abbreviations, one-letter words, the sentence dash. Dialogue and the sentence dash: fiction marks dialogue with a gondolatjel, not quotes, and a spaced gondolatjel sets off the attribution (A magyar helyesírás szabályai, 12th ed., rule 259); the rules set the en dash.

## Spaces

- One-letter words kept with the next word: none.
- Short words kept with the next word (`space.short-word`, on by default; the pack's own list): none.
- A no-break space between a number and %: yes.
- A no-break space between a number and € after it: yes.
- The space before `:` and before `; ! ?` (`space.high-punctuation`; the opt-in `space.before-punctuation` keeps it): none.
- A bare temperature without the degree sign (`20 C`) read as one (`symbol.degree`, opt-in): no.

## Notes

Swiss German quotes: `«outer ‹inner› outer»`, without spaces inside, as the Swiss Federal Chancellery Schreibweisungen set them. CLDR gives `„…“` and `‚…‘` for de-CH, so the choice stays disputed (resources/sources.md). Germany’s turned `»…«` is not read here. The abbreviation lists, the spaced en dash and the no-break space before % and € are German’s (the de pack), not checked against a Swiss guide. Digits are grouped with a no-break space (number.group): the Schreibweisungen (Rz. 512) group digits in threes with a Festabstand (no-break space). Everyday Swiss usage often writes the apostrophe (1’000’000); it is not used here (the separator is the pack field numbers.group). The decimal mark is a point (Rz. 514, 543 for francs). The Schreibweisungen page was not readable when this was written: the passage numbers (Rz. 512, 514, 543) come from its index and the PDF text, and the pack stays reviewed: false.

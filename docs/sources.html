dash.sentence

Verified

A spaced hyphen, dash or double hyphen becomes a spaced dash (em; en in German and literary British English), no-break before it

Why: A break in a sentence takes a dash, and its form depends on the language. For English the em dash is backed by the source; it is the engine default.

Others, gaps, unread: The source sets the em dash flush against the words and calls spaces acceptable; the engine sets it spaced, with a no-break space before it. The spaced en dash for German and for British literary English is not backed by any source read yet.

Sources: butterick-dashes

symbol.minus

Unchecked

A hyphen before a digit (-5) becomes a minus sign (−5)

Why: A minus sign is −, not a hyphen.

Others, gaps, unread: Unicode only shows that the character exists; it does not say when to use it. Butterick calls an en dash an acceptable minus sign in spreadsheets and math, so a style guide that prefers − over – was not found. Still unchecked.

Sources: unicode-names butterick-dashes

space.number

Verified

No-break space between a number and a short word or unit after it, up to nine letters (21 September, 5 %)

Why: A number stays on the line of its unit or month. The source backs a space between a value and a unit, percent included.

Others, gaps, unread: The source does not say the space is nonbreaking, and says nothing about dates ("21 September"); both are our choice. It spaces % in English, while CLDR sets no space there (see space.percent) and the engine follows CLDR.

Sources: nist-si-checklist

space.percent

Verified

German and French: a no-break space between a number and % or € (5 %, 49 €)

Why: German and French set a no-break space before % and a currency sign. CLDR agrees for % in de and fr, and for the currency sign in de and fr; it sets no space before % in en, uk, pl, de-CH and fr-CH, which is what the engine does.

Others, gaps, unread: CLDR also puts a no-break space before the currency sign in uk, pl and fr-CH; the engine leaves 49€ alone there (a gap, in the backlog). The pattern is how software formats a number, not a printed style guide; no printed guide (Duden, Imprimerie nationale Lexique) was read.

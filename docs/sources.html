quote

Disputed

Quotation marks by language, register and nesting level, from any source style

Why: Each language has its own quotation marks, and nesting alternates a second pair. uk (« » „ “) and pl („ ” « ») match CLDR; fr level one and ch (« » ‹ ›, as fr-CH) match too. Butterick agrees that quotes are curly and that styles vary by language, and gives no per-language sets. OQLF puts a no-break space inside French guillemets, as the engine does.

Others, gaps, unread: CLDR gives English “ ” ‘ ’ (the engine default is « » „ “, a gap already in the backlog as P0), German „ “ ‚ ‘ (the engine sets » « › ‹ on display), French « » with « » inside (the engine uses “ ” inside), and de-CH „ “ ‚ ‘ (the engine ch profile is the fr-CH one). The backlog records that every other tool in the benchmark produces “ ” for English by default (bench/RESULTS.md). Not read: the Swiss Federal Chancellery Schreibweisungen (a PDF this work could not extract text from), Pravopys 2019 (server refused the fetch), Imprimerie nationale Lexique.

apostrophe

Verified

A straight ' in a word or before a year becomes ’

Why: A typographic apostrophe is ’, not the straight typewriter mark, and the same ’ opens an elided word (’90s, rock ’n’ roll) instead of an opening quote.

Others, gaps, unread: Matches the engine, including the leading-apostrophe cases fixed in the CHANGELOG. Butterick makes an exception for the Hawaiian okina (‘Oʻahu’), which the engine does not know. English only; no source for other languages was read.

Sources: butterick-apostrophes butterick-quotes

ellipsis

Verified

... becomes …

Why: Three dots are one character, …, so they cannot break across lines.

Others, gaps, unread: Butterick agrees and also asks for a no-break space on a side with no text so the ellipsis stays with its text; the engine does not do that (`... and so` keeps an ordinary space, a gap in the backlog). UAX #14 gives … class IN, so it is never split from another ellipsis. Interrupted speech: Butterick prefers an em dash outside fiction; the engine leaves it.

dash.sentence

Verified

A spaced hyphen, dash or double hyphen becomes a spaced dash (em; en in German and literary British English), no-break before it

Why: A break in a sentence takes a dash, and its form depends on the language. For English the em dash is backed by the source; it is the engine default.

Others, gaps, unread: The source sets the em dash flush against the words and calls spaces acceptable; the engine sets it spaced, with a no-break space before it, which matches neither. A search result quoting Bringhurst (The Elements of Typographic Style, 2nd ed., section 5.2.1, not read here) says he prefers a spaced en dash in English, so the primary guides disagree among themselves. UAX #14 gives the em dash class B2, which allows a break on both sides, hence the no-break space before it. The spaced en dash for German and for British literary English is not backed by any source read yet.

Sources: butterick-dashes uax14

symbol.minus

Unchecked

A hyphen before a digit (-5) becomes a minus sign (−5)

Why: A minus sign is −, not a hyphen.

Others, gaps, unread: Unicode only shows that the character exists; it does not say when to use it. Butterick says to use an en dash for the minus sign (the "Math symbols" page also lists the &minus; entity) and calls an en dash acceptable in spreadsheets, so a style guide that prefers − over – was not found. Still unchecked.

Sources: unicode-names butterick-dashes butterick-math

symbol.multiply

Verified

The letter x between numbers (1920x1080, 3 x 4) becomes ×

Why: A dimension takes ×, not the letter x.

Others, gaps, unread: Butterick says to use × instead of the letter x, dimensions included. Unicode shows the two are different characters. The NIST SI checklist, as summarised by the fetch tool, does not address ×. English only; no source for other languages was read.

space.collapse

Verified

A run of spaces becomes one

Why: A run of spaces is a typing accident, not a setting.

Others, gaps, unread: Butterick asks for exactly one space between words and one after a full stop; the engine collapses a run to one. He offers a thin space after initials (W. A. Dwiggins); the engine uses a no-break space there (space.initial).

space.number

Verified

No-break space between a number and a short word or unit after it, up to nine letters (21 September, 5 %)

Why: A number stays on the line of its unit or month. The source backs a space between a value and a unit, percent included.

Others, gaps, unread: The source does not say the space is nonbreaking, and says nothing about dates ("21 September"); both are our choice. It spaces % in English, while CLDR sets no space there (see space.percent) and the engine follows CLDR.

Sources: nist-si-checklist uax14

space.percent

Verified

German and French: a no-break space between a number and % or € (5 %, 49 €)

Why: German and French set a no-break space before % and a currency sign. CLDR agrees for % in de and fr, and for the currency sign in de and fr; it sets no space before % in en, uk, pl, de-CH and fr-CH, which is what the engine does. OQLF, a printed-style authority, agrees for French: a no-break space before %, $ and €.

Others, gaps, unread: CLDR also puts a no-break space before the currency sign in uk, pl and fr-CH; the engine leaves 49€ alone there (a gap, in the backlog). OQLF also asks for a no-break space before a French colon, and none or a fine space before ; ! and ?, which the engine does not set (a gap, in the backlog). OQLF is a Quebec source; a France or Swiss guide may differ (a fine space). The German side rests on CLDR only; no Duden page was read.

uax14

standard

Unicode Standard Annex #14, Unicode Line Breaking Algorithm, revision 57, Unicode 18.0.0, 2026-09-01 (first 100,000 of 141,504 characters read; line-break class tables and rules LB12, LB12a not read in full).

Relied on: U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE is class GL and prohibits a break on either side, and is the preferred way to keep two words on a line (a title and a name); U+202F is the same but narrow; the horizontal ellipsis is class IN, so breaks between consecutive ellipses are never allowed.
